01 / OBJECTIVE

One route-level coefficient

The proposal computes a safety score from 0 to 100 for a route. It combines incident severity, incident age, and proximity to the route, then normalizes weighted incident risk by route distance.

02 / SPATIAL FILTERING

Count incidents near the route

An incident is included when its point is within 0.18 km of the route polyline. Distance is measured over the Earth's surface with the Haversine formula.

d = 2R · atan2(√a, √(1 − a))Great-circle distance

Here, R = 6,371 km; latitudes and longitudes are converted to radians. For two points, the intermediate value is:

a = sin²(Δφ/2) + cos(φ₁)cos(φ₂)sin²(Δλ/2)Δφ and Δλ are latitude and longitude differences

The route length is the sum of Haversine distances between consecutive route nodes. To avoid dividing by zero on very short routes, the scoring denominator is at least 1 km: Dsafe = max(D, 1.0).

Each incident’s severity weight is reduced exponentially by its age in years:

wyear = 0.8ageage = current year − incident year

The total weighted risk is the sum across qualifying incidents: W = Σ(wtype,j × wyear,j). Risk density is then R = W / Dsafe.

04 / FINAL SCORE

Map risk density to 0–100

Score = 100 × e−0.0004RProposed decay constant λ = 0.0004

The proposal says the score decreases smoothly as weighted incident density rises, approaching 100 for very low risk density and 0 for very high risk density.

This is the supplied proposal, not a description of the current code.
The implementation in app.js uses different incident weights and decay (λ = 0.001), plus a time-of-day multiplier. The overview page lists those differences. Resolve which version is authoritative before presenting the scoring model as finalized.

05 / ASSUMPTIONS & LIMITATIONS

What the model assumes

  • Incident weights add linearly; clustering does not add a separate effect.
  • Fixed severity weights represent relative danger, although the coefficients are manually selected rather than empirically calibrated.
  • Risk decays smoothly each year using a fixed 0.8 annual multiplier.
  • Hazards are treated as localized within the 0.18 km corridor.
  • Pedestrian path safety is approximated by nearby incident density per kilometer.

Possible next steps

The supplied spec suggests learning parameters from verified feedback, using live incident feeds, adding risk to routing graph edges for multi-criteria routing, and eventually evaluating data-driven prediction. These are roadmap ideas, not current features.
